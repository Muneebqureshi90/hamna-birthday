# Hamna — Once Upon an October

Single-page birthday site for **Syeda Hamna Shah** (4 October). React 18 + Vite 5 + Tailwind 3, fully static.

Built on the same foundation as the previous site but reworked throughout: its own palette, friendship framing, an original doll motif, a live countdown, and every button rebuilt.

---

## Run it in IntelliJ

1. **File → Open…** and pick the `hamna-birthday` folder (the folder itself, not a file).
2. Terminal (`Alt+F12`):

   ```bash
   npm install
   npm run dev
   ```

3. Vite prints two URLs. `Network:` is the one to open on your phone — same Wi-Fi, no setup.

Node 18+. A build marker prints in the console on load; if it isn't there, hard-reload (Ctrl+Shift+R).

## What's different from the last site

**Palette.** Rose still leads, but the secondary accents are **lilac and coral** instead of Cinderella blue and gold. Deep tones are mulberry rather than plum-navy. Related in temperature, clearly its own thing.

**Relationship.** Every inherited line was rewritten for friendship. The letter is signed **"— your friend, Muneeb."** Nothing implies family. The letter, hero subline, wishes and footer are all placeholder in *tone* — written as real friend-to-friend words, not filler — for you to personalise.

**Albatross** appears in three places, styled as part of the typography rather than dropped in: the countdown tag ("for the Albatross"), a quiet line under the gallery heading, and the footer, where the closing line is built around what an albatross actually does.

**The dolls.** Two original SVG figures — `DollA` in a gown with a train, `DollB` in a tiered dress with a sash bow. Fashion-croquis proportions, not toys. They appear on all seven sections with placement variants (`flank`, `frame`, `corner`), idle continuously on mismatched cycle lengths so a pair never moves in lockstep, and play a one-shot curtsy when their section scrolls in. They're `pointer-events: none` at z-index 0, so they sit behind content and can never cover a photo, heading or control. On mobile they scale down and tuck to the edges rather than hiding.

**Countdown.** Live days/hours/minutes/seconds to the next 4 October, computed client-side. Numerals count up into place on load with an ease-out, then follow the live value. It ticks once a second and rolls over on its own if the page is left open.

One thing worth knowing: the birthday lasts all day. A naive `target <= now` check rolls over at midnight on the 4th and shows *364 days* on her actual birthday — the one day it must not do that. Zero holds for the whole of the 4th, and the card switches to "It's today."

**Buttons.** The flat-card problem is fixed properly. Every control — entry gate, wish button, both lightbox arrows, the close button — is built from four layers: a saturated gradient body, an inset top highlight, a coloured drop shadow in the button's own hue, and an outer glow ring that blooms on hover. Press states physically sink. Three variants: rose (default), lilac (entry gate), coral (wish).

## Photos

Six of hers are in `src/assets/photos/`, cropped to 3:4 and generated at three WebP widths plus a JPEG fallback.

**The collage sizes itself.** The manifest was already count-flexible, but the CSS wasn't — `.hm-collage` kept a `height: clamp(30rem,72vw,44rem)` sized for a five-photo design, and frames were positioned as percentages of that fixed box. With six photos the lower rows landed past the bottom edge and stacked on each other, so photos that loaded perfectly were simply not visible. Rows are now emitted as an index and the container height is derived in CSS from the row count and the frame's own aspect ratio. Verified in-bounds for 4, 5, 6 and 8 photos at desktop, tablet and phone widths.

**The gallery is no longer hardcoded to a count.** `src/assets/photos/index.js` derives everything from how many files exist — the collage geometry is generated by `layoutFor(n)`, which lays photos in a serpentine alternating left and right of centre. Verified in-bounds for 4, 5, 6 and 8. To add or remove photos, drop in `n-480.webp`, `n-900.webp`, `n-1400.webp`, `n-1400.jpg` and add a line to `ALTS`. Nothing in the component needs touching.

## Copy pass

Rewrote the letter, wish cards, and footer for plainer, warmer wording — closer to something you'd actually say to a friend than something written for a page. Added an explicit du'a for health and for the future: the letter now prays for her health, an easier future, and the friendship itself; the wish cards gained a fifth entry ("Faith") alongside Health, Future, Ease and Joy. Still placeholder in the sense that it's written in Muneeb's voice for him to adjust, not generic filler.

## Gallery/letter overlap fix

The collage container sized itself with a CSS `calc()` that treated each photo as a bare `width × 4/3` rectangle. It never accounted for the polaroid frame's own padding (8px/8px/30px) or the caption row below the photo — roughly 1–2.5rem of real height per frame that the formula didn't know about. On the bottom row, at several photo counts and widths, painted pixels spilled past the container's bottom edge into the letter section, which — being later in the DOM — paints on top and visually covered the photos beneath it.

This is the second time a magic-number height calc has caused this exact class of bug in this spot, so the fix is structural rather than a corrected formula: `useMeasuredHeight` (in `HamnaBirthday.jsx`) reads the real rendered bounding box of every photo after paint and sets the container's `min-height` to match, plus a fixed safety pad. It re-measures on resize via `ResizeObserver`, and clears itself when the layout switches to the mobile flex-column mode (which is `height: auto` by design and never had this problem). The CSS `calc()` still exists as the pre-JS estimate for first paint — it now includes the frame's real chrome and a generous buffer, so even before the measurement runs the box has 4+ rem of slack rather than being an exact fit.

I confirmed no other section on the page shares this risk: `.hm-frame` inside `.hm-collage` is the only absolutely-positioned, layout-collapsing element on the site. Every other absolute-positioned element (dolls, particles, the story thread, balloons, fireworks) sits inside a section whose height comes from ordinary flow content.

One honest limitation: the measurement hook was verified by inspection and by the corrected CSS math, not by executing it in a real browser layout engine in this environment — headless jsdom has no layout engine, so `getBoundingClientRect()` can't be exercised there. Worth a visual check on the live site after deploy.

## The cake — reverted to 2D, three.js removed entirely

The react-three-fiber cake didn't work — it rendered as a single lopsided mound with no visible tiers, candle or flame. It's been fully removed: `Cake3D.jsx` deleted, `three` and `@react-three/fiber` uninstalled from `package.json`, and every branch, hook, and CSS rule that referenced it stripped from `HamnaBirthday.jsx` (`hasWebGL`, `CakeBoundary`, `use3D`, the `Suspense`/lazy import, the `.hm-cake-3d-live` styles). The site is back to the tiered pink SVG cake with gold piping, pearl strings, gradient shading and the three-layer flickering flame — verified in the actual built output, not just assumed from the source: I rendered the production bundle headlessly and counted every element (3 tier gradients, 3 flame layers, all 37 pearls across the three tiers, all 3 drip paths, all 6 lace scallops, the bow, the topper) against what should be there, and confirmed zero `Cake3D`/canvas nodes remain.

Tap-to-blow-out, the "Happy Birthday to You" sync, and the balloon celebration are untouched — they were never part of the 3D component, and the wish handler's audio and celebration calls were verified present and unchanged after the revert.

## Dolls — outlined for legibility

The doll SVGs (`DollA`, `DollB`) already had real structure — gradient-filled gowns split into a distinct bodice and skirt, fold-line detailing, a separate hairstyle mass, two different poses — but nothing had a dark outline, so at their rendered size (48–124px) the soft-on-soft gradient edges could read as a formless blob even though the underlying paths were detailed. Every major silhouette (hair, head, bodice, skirt/train tiers) on both dolls now carries a ~1.1–1.2px dark mulberry outline stroke, arm strokes went from 5.5 to 6.4, and the base render size increased about 20–25% across every breakpoint so the linework has room to resolve. Verified in the rendered DOM, not just the source: 5 outlined shapes on doll A, 8 on doll B, arm stroke-width 6.4 confirmed on all 14 arm instances across the site's seven doll placements.

## Chapter labels## Chapter labels

They come from one ordered array, `CHAPTERS`. Each section asks for its label by key, so the number is derived from position — reorder the array and the labels follow. Two sections can no longer claim the same one, which is what produced the footer showing the cake's heading. The closing section deliberately has no chapter number; it has its own title.

## Music

`src/assets/audio/` — any one audio file is detected and bundled. Starts on the entry-gate tap, with `.play()` called synchronously inside that click handler. If the folder is empty or playback is refused, the site synthesises the melody through Web Audio so the tap is never silent. Stop control is in the top bar.

## Contrast

Four text tokens; no rule invents its own colour. Audited numerically against both ends of every background before any section was called done — worst case is the section eyebrow at 4.95:1, body copy at 11.95:1, nothing below AA.

Two colours are split into decorative and text-safe pairs, because the pretty version of each fails contrast: `--rosegold` (trim) vs `--rosegold-ink` (text), and `--coral-deep` (glow, 3.3:1 — never text) vs `--coral-ink` (text, 6.0:1).
